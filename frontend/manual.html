Purpose. MIFE is a research-based decision-support tool for understanding weather-driven Amblypelta population phenology and relative pressure. It supports—but does not replace—field monitoring, crop-stage assessment, professional judgement or label-compliant pest management.

How to use the dashboard

  1. Weather series: choose the closest appropriate regional series. Numbered sites are de-identified regional series, not exact orchard stations.
  2. Weather input: choose Stored research record for reproducible retrospective runs or Live regional weather for the recent/forecast window.
  3. Reference starting adults: retain 100 for routine use. It controls scale internally; the displayed phenology is normalized.
  4. Period: select dates within the available range. Live daily weather extends only through the provider's forecast horizon.
  5. Macadamia crop stage: select the closest grower-observed BBCH group. This controls only the crop-overlap interpretation and does not change insect biology or the forecast curve.
  6. Click Run nine-scenario forecast, then examine both chart tabs and the interpretation text.
Do not choose Malua, Knockrow or Dorey as if they were live orchard forecasts. They are named validation series retained for scientific comparison. Live weather is restricted to representative regional series.

What the model does

MIFE is a deterministic, daily, stage-structured population model. It follows eggs, five nymphal instars and age-structured female and male adults. Each day it combines weather with measured biological schedules to update development, survival, adult ageing and reproduction.

ComponentImplementationMeaning
Immature developmentTemperature-dependent stage duration; linear interpolation between measured temperatures. Four points approximate a fluctuating daily temperature curve.Warmer or cooler days alter physiological progress through Egg and N1–N5.
Immature survivalStage- and temperature-specific conditional survival interpolated from the frozen parameter tables.Only a proportion progresses successfully through each stage.
Adult survivalDaily age- and temperature-specific conditional survival for females and males, with daily age classes.Adult persistence depends on both age and temperature.
FecundityFemale-age and temperature-dependent egg production. Reproduction is zero at or below 15°C and at or above 35°C in the frozen model.Female reproductive output changes through adult life and with temperature.
Seasonal activationThree preregistered smooth day-length profiles: conservative, central and permissive.Represents uncertainty in winter reproductive suppression without forcing reproduction to start on a calendar date.
InitializationOverwintering adults distributed across young, mixed and older adult-age bands.Represents uncertainty in the physiological age of adults entering the annual run.

Temperatures outside measured immature-development and survival ranges are clamped to the nearest observed boundary rather than extrapolated. The seasonal activation curves are uncertainty scenarios, not fitted photoperiod parameters and not evidence of diapause.

Why nine scenarios?

The dashboard combines three seasonal-activation profiles with three overwintering-adult age bands, producing nine predefined scenarios. All nine are reported together; none is selected because it fits a particular orchard observation best.

The dark chart line is the median of the nine scenarios. The shaded area is their full range. It is a sensitivity envelope, not a statistical confidence interval.

Autumn carryover forecast: routine field observations ended when further damage was no longer economically meaningful for that crop cycle. MIFE deliberately continues through the unsampled months to simulate seasonal carryover and overwintering population development. A March–April maximum is biologically plausible because an autumn generation is documented and fruitspotting bugs can remain present into April, but it is a modelled extrapolation and not a direct treatment signal. The primary grower interpretation is population pressure during susceptible reproductive crop stages.

Macadamia BBCH crop-stage guide

The dashboard uses a simplified reproductive macadamia BBCH grouping to describe crop tissue relevant to fruitspotting bug risk. BBCH 00–39 is intentionally omitted because vegetative and dormant stages are not a useful attack-risk selection for this grower forecast. Select the stage that best represents the orchard. If stages differ among cultivars, blocks or parts of a tree, choose Unknown or run separate interpretations.

The stage codes are useful for standardization, but its calendar timing must not be transferred directly to Australian cultivars or regions.

Field sampling estimate

Enter the number of Amblypelta collected and the number of trees sampled using one comparable standardized sample per tree. Enter known trees/ha, or calculate planting density from spacing:

Trees/ha = 10,000 ÷ (row spacing in m × within-row tree spacing in m)
Sampling-equivalent bugs/ha = (bugs collected ÷ trees sampled) × trees/ha

Example: 3 bugs from 30 sampled trees at 8 m × 4 m spacing gives 312.5 trees/ha and 31.25 sampling-equivalent bugs/ha.

This assumes sampled trees represent the orchard and sampling effort/detection are comparable. It does not correct for insects missed elsewhere in the canopy.

Weather and location guidance

  • Stored weather is the reproducible baseline used for retrospective analysis.
  • Live regional weather merges recent and forecast daily minimum temperature, maximum temperature and precipitation from Open-Meteo at representative regional coordinates.
  • The live coordinate is a regional environmental proxy, not an inferred private orchard location.
  • Weather data alone do not represent immigration, insecticide effects, host movement, orchard-edge effects or local microclimate.
  • Queensland regional application should be treated as exploratory where local species composition or ecology differs from the Northern NSW evidence base.

Validation status and limitations

MIFE v0.1 was initially compared with 21 field events: seven each from Malua, Knockrow and Dorey. The expanded evidence review now contains 156 de-identified orchard/block-date events spanning 2023–24 to 2025–26 across Northern NSW, southeast Queensland and central Queensland. The original 21 events are embedded in that archive and are not counted again as new evidence.

The 18 Northern NSW events from 2025–26 were reserved as an independent out-of-time test. Five events contained confirmed FSB/BSB, totalling seven confirmed individuals. Across the frozen nine-scenario ensemble, preliminary pooled discrimination between confirmed detection and non-detection was AUC 0.731. This result is encouraging but uncertain because there were only five positive events, management is a potential confounder and sampling ended in February.

  • The model is strongest as a weather-driven phenology and relative-pressure prototype.
  • It is not calibrated to absolute orchard abundance.
  • The closed population engine has no active immigration/emigration term.
  • It cannot distinguish locally surviving adults from adults moving into an orchard.
  • No consistent economic threshold is encoded.
  • March–May standardized sampling is the priority for testing the autumn peak.
Interpretation boundary: AUC 0.731 is a preliminary diagnostic statistic, not proof of operational accuracy and not a pesticide-action threshold. The completed data still do not directly test the modelled March–May peak.

No thermal-development, survival, fecundity, seasonal-activation or other frozen biological parameter was changed in response to either validation exercise.

Decision guidelines

  • Confirm field presence and fresh damage; do not act on the forecast alone.
  • Consider crop stage and tissue susceptibility separately from population pressure.
  • Review recent insecticide applications, weather, orchard edges/hotspots and nearby alternative hosts.
  • Use registered products strictly according to the current label or permit and seek qualified agronomic advice.
  • Consider pollinators, parasitoids and predators before disruptive actions.
  • Record observations so future versions can be independently validated.
MIFE does not provide a pesticide recommendation and does not replace professional judgement.
