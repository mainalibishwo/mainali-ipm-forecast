Research-based decision support. This tool shows when spotting bug populations may be building, declining or approaching their seasonal peak, helping growers decide when to increase orchard monitoring.

What this forecast offers

MIFE combines regional temperature and rainfall with spotting bug biology to forecast population timing and relative build-up. It is designed to support monitoring—not to recommend an insecticide treatment.

Species coveredOne combined Amblypelta forecast is shown for fruitspotting bug (FSB, A. nitida) and banana spotting bug (BSB, A. lutescens lutescens). Separate species curves are not currently produced. Most field validation evidence is for FSB, so BSB-specific performance remains less certain.
What the 0–100 index meansThe index has no units. For each model scenario, 100 is its highest predicted nymph-plus-adult activity during the displayed period. Lower values indicate lower activity relative to that seasonal peak.
How growers can use itCheck whether regional activity is increasing, compare it with the orchard’s current crop stage, and use the result to decide whether routine or more intensive field monitoring is appropriate.

Seasonal population activity (0–100 index)

Predicted seasonal peak window—Timing of the highest modelled nymph-plus-adult activity—not automatically the highest crop-risk period.
How to read this graph: 100 marks the highest modelled nymph-plus-adult activity in each scenario during the displayed period; it is not a bug count. The dark line is the middle result across nine biological scenarios, and the green band shows their full range. Pale blue indicates future weather; the dashed line marks today.
Modelled relative composition. Eggs, nymphs and adults are component-wise scenario medians rescaled to total 100% each day. They are not observed counts, bugs per tree or bugs per hectare.
